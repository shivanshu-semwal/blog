## Early Days: The Era of Punch Cards and Line Editors

In the early days of computing, programming was done using punch cards or paper tape. Editing involved manually rearranging cards or overwriting data.

As terminal systems emerged, editors like `ed` and `TECO` appeared.

## The Rise of Screen-Based Editors

With video display terminals, editors moved from line-based to screen-based editing.

Examples include:

- `Vi`
- `Emacs`

## Personal Computers and User-Friendly Editors

Editors became more user-friendly for non-programmers.

Examples:

- WordStar
- MS-DOS Editor
- Notepad

## IDEs and specialized editors

The 1990s brought IDEs for programmers as well as more powerful editors.

Examples:

- Vim
- Sublime Text

## Modern Era

Modern editors focus on performance, extensibility, and collaboration.

Examples:

- Atom
- VS Code
- collaborative online editors
